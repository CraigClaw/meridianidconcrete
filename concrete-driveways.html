New Concrete Driveway Installation

A new driveway is one of the best investments you can make in your property. Our installation process is thorough and designed for Idaho's unique climate conditions. We start with proper site preparation — excavating to the correct depth, installing a compacted gravel base, and ensuring proper drainage away from your home's foundation. This base preparation is critical in Meridian, where expansive clay soils can shift and crack poorly installed concrete.

We use 4,000 PSI concrete with proper air entrainment (5-7%) to withstand the freeze-thaw cycles that are common in the Boise metro area. Control joints are placed at strategic intervals to manage cracking naturally. Every driveway we pour includes reinforced wire mesh or fiber reinforcement for added structural integrity.

Driveway Replacement & Removal

If your existing driveway is crumbling, severely cracked, or sinking, replacement is often more cost-effective than ongoing repairs. Our replacement process includes complete removal and disposal of the old concrete, reassessment of the base material, and installation of a brand-new driveway with modern specifications. Many older driveways in Meridian neighborhoods like Paramount and Settlers Bridge were poured without adequate base preparation — we make sure the new driveway is built to last.

Concrete vs. Asphalt Driveways in Idaho

Many Meridian homeowners ask whether concrete or asphalt is the better choice. Here's the reality for the Boise metro area: concrete lasts 25-30 years with proper maintenance, while asphalt typically needs replacement every 15-20 years. Concrete also handles Idaho's temperature swings better — asphalt softens in summer heat and becomes brittle in sub-zero winter temperatures. While asphalt may have a lower upfront cost, concrete's longer lifespan and lower maintenance requirements make it the better long-term investment for Treasure Valley homeowners.

Driveway Resurfacing Options

If your existing concrete driveway is structurally sound but showing surface wear, resurfacing can give it new life at a fraction of the replacement cost. Our resurfacing process involves cleaning the existing surface, repairing minor cracks and spalling, and applying a thin polymer-modified concrete overlay. This overlay can be finished with the same stamped and decorative options as a new pour, completely transforming the look of your driveway without the expense of full replacement.

Maintaining Your Concrete Driveway in Idaho

Idaho's freeze-thaw cycles are tough on concrete. We recommend sealing your driveway every 2-3 years with a high-quality penetrating sealer. Avoid using de-icing salts — they cause surface scaling and can accelerate freeze-thaw damage. Instead, use sand for traction on icy patches. Keep joints clear of debris, and address any cracks promptly before water infiltration makes them worse.

How long before I can drive on my new concrete driveway?
We recommend waiting at least 7 days before driving on new concrete. Full strength develops over 28 days, but after 7 days the concrete has reached approximately 70% strength. We'll advise you on proper curing times based on weather conditions at the time of your pour.
