Whether you are looking to add a simple broom-finish patio for weekend barbecues in your Meridian neighborhood near Settlers Park, a stamped concrete masterpiece for your Eagle estate in the Foothills, or a multi-level patio with fire pit integration for your Boise Bench home with city views, our team delivers quality craftsmanship that lasts for decades. Fire Pit Integration and Outdoor Kitchen Foundations

Outdoor fire features and cooking areas have become essential elements of Treasure Valley outdoor living. We specialize in pouring the concrete foundations that make these features possible:

Fire Pit Pads: We pour heat-rated concrete pads for both built-in and standalone fire pits. These pads use special concrete mixes with additional reinforcement to withstand high temperatures without cracking. We can integrate a fire pit into your existing patio by cutting out a section and pouring a new pad, or build a standalone fire pit area adjacent to your patio — perfect for those cool Idaho autumn evenings when you want to extend your outdoor season.

Outdoor Kitchen Foundations: Outdoor kitchens require a solid, level concrete slab that can support the weight of built-in grills, refrigerators, countertops, and storage cabinets. We pour reinforced slabs with proper thickness (typically 4-6 inches) and ensure the foundation extends to accommodate all planned features. We coordinate with your outdoor kitchen contractor to ensure utilities (gas, electric, water) are properly routed before the pour.

Pergola and Cover Footings: If you are planning to add a pergola, pavilion, or covered structure over your patio for shade during hot July afternoons, we can pour the footings and integrate them with your patio slab for a seamless look. Proper footings are essential for structural stability, especially in Idaho's freeze-thaw climate where ground movement can compromise inadequate foundations.

Concrete Patio Cost in Meridian and Boise, Idaho

Understanding patio costs helps you budget appropriately for your outdoor living project. Here are typical price ranges for the Treasure Valley:

  • Standard Broom Finish: $6-$10 per square foot. A typical 400 sq ft patio costs $2,400-$4,000.
  • Stamped Concrete: $10-$18 per square foot. A 400 sq ft stamped patio ranges from $4,000-$7,200.
  • Stained Concrete: $8-$14 per square foot for acid staining. Water-based stains and decorative effects can run $12-$20 per square foot.
  • Exposed Aggregate: $8-$14 per square foot, depending on the aggregate size and exposure level.

Additional costs may include excavation and removal of existing surfaces ($1-$3 per sq ft), steps and grade changes ($50-$100 per step), fire pit pads ($300-$600), built-in seating walls ($150-$250 per linear foot), and decorative borders ($3-$6 per linear foot). Maintaining Your Concrete Patio in Idaho's Climate

Idaho's climate is tough on concrete patios. Intense UV exposure at our elevation, 100+ freeze-thaw cycles annually, and temperature swings from sub-zero winters to 100+ degree summers all take their toll. Proper maintenance extends your patio's life and keeps it looking beautiful for decades:

Seal Every 2-3 Years: UV rays break down sealers faster at Idaho's elevation (approximately 2,700 feet in Meridian). Use a high-quality penetrating silane/siloxane sealer that protects against freeze-thaw damage without creating a slippery surface. Budget $200-$400 for resealing a typical patio.

Avoid De-Icing Salts: Sodium chloride and calcium chloride destroy concrete sealer and cause surface scaling. Use sand for traction on icy patches instead. If you must use a de-icer, choose a concrete-safe magnesium chloride product sparingly.

Clean Spills Promptly: BBQ grease, wine, fertilizer, and other substances can stain unsealed or worn concrete. Clean spills with a mild concrete cleaner and a soft brush. Avoid harsh acids that can etch the surface.

Protect Furniture: Metal furniture legs can rust and stain concrete, especially when wet. Use furniture pads or lift furniture during winter months. Avoid dragging heavy objects across the surface.

Keep Joints Clear: Control joints allow concrete to expand and contract naturally with temperature changes. Keep them clear of debris, dirt, and vegetation to prevent water from pooling and causing damage during freeze-thaw cycles.
