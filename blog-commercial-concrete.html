Commercial Concrete Services We Provide

Our commercial concrete capabilities span a wide range of applications for businesses, municipalities, and industrial clients:

Parking Lots and Drive Aisles: From small retail parking areas to large commercial lots, we design and install concrete parking surfaces built to withstand constant vehicle traffic, heavy delivery trucks, and Idaho's freeze-thaw cycles. Our commercial parking lots feature proper drainage, appropriate thickness (typically 6–8 inches for cars, 8–10 inches for truck traffic), and control joints that minimize random cracking.

Sidewalks and ADA Ramps: Compliant pedestrian access is essential for commercial properties. We install sidewalks meeting ADA requirements for width (minimum 36 inches), slope (maximum 2% cross-slope, 5% running slope), and surface texture. Our ADA ramp installations meet all current standards for slope, landing areas, and detectable warning surfaces.

Loading Docks and Truck Aprons: Heavy-duty concrete for commercial loading areas requires specialized design. We install reinforced slabs with appropriate thickness (10–12 inches or more), dowel joints for load transfer, and durable finishes that withstand constant forklift and truck traffic.

Building Foundations and Slabs: From small retail buildings to warehouses and industrial facilities, we pour commercial-grade foundations and floor slabs. Our work includes vapor barriers, reinforcement per structural engineer specifications, and finishes appropriate for the intended use (warehouse floors, retail spaces, office buildings).

Curbs and Gutters: Commercial properties require proper curbing for drainage, landscaping definition, and vehicle containment. We install monolithic curb and gutter, separated curb and gutter, and decorative curbing for commercial landscaping.

Concrete Repair and Maintenance: Existing commercial concrete needs ongoing maintenance. We provide crack repair, joint sealing, slab jacking for settled concrete, and partial or full replacement of deteriorated sections. Our commercial repair work minimizes business disruption with efficient scheduling and phased work when needed.

Decorative Commercial Concrete: First impressions matter for businesses. Stamped concrete entries, colored concrete plazas, and decorative walkways enhance curb appeal while providing durable, low-maintenance surfaces. We work with architects and designers to achieve specific aesthetic goals.

Why Choose Concrete for Commercial Applications?

While asphalt is common for commercial parking lots, concrete offers compelling advantages for many business applications:

Longer Lifespan: Commercial concrete parking lots and driveways last 25–40 years with proper maintenance, compared to 15–20 years for asphalt. This reduced replacement frequency translates to lower lifecycle costs.

Lower Maintenance: Concrete doesn't require regular sealcoating like asphalt. Routine maintenance consists primarily of joint sealing every 3–5 years and occasional crack repair. This means less ongoing expense and less disruption to your business operations.

Better Durability: Concrete resists damage from fuel spills, oil leaks, and UV degradation better than asphalt. It doesn't soften in Idaho's summer heat, preventing ruts from heavy vehicles and shopping cart marks.

Improved Lighting: Concrete's light color reflects more light than dark asphalt, improving visibility and potentially reducing lighting costs for evening operations. This also contributes to improved safety and security.

Professional Appearance: Concrete maintains a clean, professional appearance throughout its lifespan. Asphalt fades, develops oil stains, and shows tire marks more prominently.

Environmental Benefits: Concrete's reflectivity reduces heat island effect. Its longevity means less frequent reconstruction and associated environmental impact. Concrete is also fully recyclable at end of life.

Understanding Commercial Concrete Specifications

Commercial projects typically involve detailed specifications that differ from residential work:

Concrete Strength: Commercial slabs typically specify 4,000–5,000 PSI compressive strength (vs. 3,000–3,500 PSI for residential). Heavy industrial applications may require 6,000+ PSI.

Thickness Requirements: Commercial parking lots: 6–8 inches minimum. Truck traffic areas: 8–10 inches. Loading docks and industrial floors: 10–12 inches or as engineered. Heavy industrial: 12+ inches with specialized reinforcement.

Reinforcement: Commercial work typically requires welded wire mesh, fiber reinforcement, or rebar grids per structural engineer specifications. Post-tension cables may be specified for large slabs or problematic soils.

Subbase Preparation: Commercial projects require engineered subbase — typically 6–12 inches of compacted gravel base with proof rolling to verify compaction. Poor Treasure Valley clay soils may require soil stabilization or over-excavation and replacement.

Jointing: Control joints are critical for commercial slabs to control cracking. Joint spacing, depth, and timing follow ACI (American Concrete Institute) guidelines. Saw-cut joints are typically required for commercial work.

Finishes: Commercial finishes vary by application — broom finish for exterior walkways and parking areas, steel trowel for interior floors, special finishes for warehouses (hardener toppings, sealers, polishing).

ADA Compliance for Commercial Properties

Commercial properties must comply with Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) requirements. We ensure all our commercial work meets current ADA standards:

  • Accessible Routes: Minimum 36-inch width, maximum 5% running slope, maximum 2% cross-slope
  • Ramps: Maximum 8.33% slope (1:12), required handrails for ramps over 6 inches, level landings at top, bottom, and direction changes
  • Parking: Required number of accessible spaces based on total parking count, proper striping and signage, access aisles (minimum 60 inches for cars, 96 inches for vans)
  • Detectable Warnings: Truncated dome surfaces at curb ramps and hazardous vehicular areas
  • Surface Requirements: Firm, stable, slip-resistant surfaces without gaps or abrupt changes in level

Non-compliant ADA features can result in lawsuits and costly retrofits. We stay current on ADA requirements and ensure all commercial installations meet or exceed standards.
