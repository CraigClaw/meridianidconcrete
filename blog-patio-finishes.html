Your concrete patio is more than just a slab—it's an extension of your living space, a place for family gatherings, outdoor dining, and relaxing summer evenings. The finish you choose affects not only appearance but also slip resistance, maintenance requirements, and long-term durability. This comprehensive guide walks you through the most popular concrete patio finishes available to Treasure Valley homeowners, helping you make the right choice for your outdoor space.

Understanding Concrete Finishes

A concrete finish refers to the texture and appearance of the surface after the concrete has been poured and partially set. The finish is applied while the concrete is still workable but firm enough to hold the pattern or texture. Your choice of finish impacts:

  • Appearance: From simple and functional to decorative and elegant
  • Slip resistance: Critical for pool decks and areas that get wet
  • Maintenance: Some finishes hide dirt better than others
  • Cost: Decorative finishes add to the base concrete price
  • Comfort: Texture affects barefoot comfort and furniture placement

Finish #1: Broom Finish (Textured)

The most common and economical concrete finish, broom finish is created by dragging a stiff-bristled broom across the surface while the concrete is still wet.

Appearance: Subtle linear texture with a matte, non-reflective surface. Available in standard gray or integral colors.

Pros:

  • Most affordable finish option
  • Excellent slip resistance, even when wet
  • Hides footprints and minor imperfections well
  • Low maintenance—easy to clean with pressure washing
  • Timeless, classic appearance that works with any home style
  • Quick to install—no additional time added to pour day

Cons:

  • Limited decorative appeal—basic and functional
  • Texture can be rough on bare feet
  • Difficult to achieve perfectly smooth seating areas for furniture
  • Fewer design options compared to decorative finishes

Best For: Budget-conscious homeowners, pool decks (slip resistance), high-traffic patios, traditional home styles, rental properties.

Cost Range: $6–$8 per square foot (included in standard concrete pricing)

Idaho Considerations: Broom finish performs well in freeze-thaw conditions. The texture provides grip during icy conditions, though you'll still want to use ice melt carefully in winter.

Finish #2: Stamped Concrete

Stamped concrete uses textured mats pressed into the surface to create patterns that mimic brick, slate, flagstone, wood planks, cobblestone, and other materials.

Appearance: Highly decorative with realistic patterns and colors. Can be customized to match your home's architecture and landscaping.

Popular Stamp Patterns in Treasure Valley:

  • Ashlar Slate: Rectangular stone pattern, very popular for modern homes
  • Cobblestone: Classic European look, works well with traditional architecture
  • Flagstone: Natural stone appearance with irregular shapes
  • Wood Plank: Mimics hardwood or decking, great for outdoor living spaces
  • Fan Pattern: Elegant curved design, popular for formal patios

Pros:

  • Unlimited design possibilities and color combinations
  • Mimics expensive materials (natural stone, brick) at fraction of cost
  • Increases property value and curb appeal
  • Seamless surface—no weeds growing between pavers
  • Can incorporate borders, accents, and custom designs
  • Durable and long-lasting when properly installed and sealed

Cons:

  • Higher cost than basic finishes
  • Requires resealing every 2–3 years to maintain appearance
  • Can be slippery when wet (requires non-slip sealer additive)
  • More complex installation requires experienced contractors
  • Pattern may show wear in high-traffic areas over time

Best For: Homeowners seeking decorative appeal, outdoor kitchens and entertaining spaces, homes where curb appeal matters, those wanting natural stone look without stone cost.

Cost Range: $12–$18 per square foot (includes stamping, color, and initial sealing)

Idaho Considerations: Stamped concrete must be properly sealed to survive freeze-thaw cycles. We use penetrating sealers with non-slip additives for Idaho patios. Avoid de-icing salts in winter, which can damage the sealer.

Finish #3: Exposed Aggregate

Exposed aggregate finish reveals the decorative stones within the concrete by washing away the top layer of cement paste before the concrete fully cures.

Appearance: Natural, textured surface with visible stones in various colors and sizes. Each patio is unique based on the aggregate selected.

Aggregate Options:

  • Natural river rock: Smooth, rounded stones in earth tones
  • Crushed granite: Angular stones with sparkle, available in various colors
  • Colored glass: Decorative option for contemporary designs
  • Recycled materials: Eco-friendly option with unique appearance

Pros:

  • Natural, organic appearance that complements landscaping
  • Excellent slip resistance—one of the safest options when wet
  • Hides dirt, stains, and wear very well
  • Extremely durable—stones protect the surface
  • Cooler surface temperature than smooth finishes (stones don't absorb as much heat)
  • Low maintenance—occasional sealing and cleaning

Cons:

  • Rough texture can be uncomfortable for bare feet
  • Not ideal for furniture that needs to slide or for children's play areas
  • More expensive than broom finish
  • Difficult to repair if damaged (patches will be visible)
  • Limited color control—depends on aggregate selection

Best For: Pool decks, walkways, homeowners wanting natural appearance, high-traffic areas, those prioritizing slip resistance.

Cost Range: $10–$14 per square foot

Idaho Considerations: Exposed aggregate performs exceptionally well in Idaho climate. The texture provides grip in icy conditions, and the durable surface resists freeze-thaw damage. Choose angular aggregate for better interlock and durability.

Finish #4: Stained Concrete (Acid or Water-Based)

Concrete staining uses chemical or penetrating dyes to add rich, variegated color to the concrete surface. Often combined with other finishes.

Appearance: Translucent, marble-like color variations. Each patio is unique with organic color patterns that cannot be exactly replicated.

Stain Types:

  • Acid stains: Chemical reaction creates earthy, mottled colors (browns, tans, terra cottas, soft blues)
  • Water-based dyes: Penetrating dyes offer wider color range including brighter options
  • Acetone dyes: Vibrant colors with translucent appearance

Pros:

  • Unique, one-of-a-kind appearance—no two patios look exactly alike
  • Color penetrates the surface—won't peel or flake like paint
  • Can be applied to existing concrete (great for patio updates)
  • Wide range of color options and effects
  • Can be combined with stamping, scoring, or other textures
  • Low maintenance with periodic resealing

Cons:

  • Color results can be unpredictable—exact match not possible
  • Requires skilled applicator for best results
  • Must be sealed regularly to maintain color vibrancy
  • Existing concrete condition affects final appearance
  • Higher cost than basic finishes

Best For: Homeowners wanting unique appearance, updating existing patios, Mediterranean or Southwestern style homes, artistic/custom designs.

Cost Range: $8–$15 per square foot (staining only; existing concrete) or $10–$18 per square foot (new concrete with stain)

Idaho Considerations: Stained concrete must be properly sealed for outdoor use in Idaho. UV exposure can fade some stains over time, so high-quality UV-resistant sealers are essential. Reapply sealer every 2–3 years.

Finish #5: Salt Finish

Salt finish creates a subtle, speckled texture by pressing rock salt into the concrete surface and then washing it out after the concrete sets.

Appearance: Fine, uniform texture with small dimples throughout. Elegant and understated with a slight sparkle when sealed.

Pros:

  • Subtle, elegant appearance that works with any architectural style
  • Good slip resistance without being rough
  • Comfortable for bare feet (smoother than exposed aggregate)
  • Hides minor imperfections and footprints well
  • Relatively low maintenance
  • More affordable than stamped concrete

Cons:

  • Limited decorative appeal compared to stamped options
  • Texture can trap dirt in the dimples
  • Requires skilled application for consistent results
  • Not as commonly offered—requires experienced contractors

Best For: Homeowners wanting subtle texture, pool decks, contemporary home styles, those who find broom finish too rough but want slip resistance.

Cost Range: $9–$12 per square foot

Idaho Considerations: Salt finish performs well in freeze-thaw conditions. The texture provides adequate slip resistance for most conditions. Seal regularly to protect the surface and maintain appearance.

Factors to Consider When Choosing

1. Budget: Determine your total patio budget, then allocate appropriately. Remember that decorative finishes add value to your home, potentially offsetting their higher cost.

2. Usage: Will the patio host large gatherings? Need to accommodate children or elderly family members? Pool access? Your usage patterns should guide your finish choice.

3. Home Architecture: Your patio finish should complement your home's style. Stamped cobblestone suits traditional homes, while smooth stained concrete works with contemporary architecture.

4. Maintenance Commitment: Be honest about how much maintenance you'll actually do. If you won't reseal every 2–3 years, choose a lower-maintenance option.

5. Climate Considerations: Idaho's freeze-thaw cycles demand proper installation regardless of finish. All outdoor concrete must be air-entrained and properly sealed.

Our Recommendation for Treasure Valley Patios

After 15+ years serving the Boise metro area, we've seen what works best for Idaho homeowners:

  • Best Overall Value: Stamped concrete with ashlar slate or flagstone pattern. The decorative appeal increases home value while providing decades of service.
  • Best for Pool Decks: Exposed aggregate or textured broom finish for maximum slip resistance.
  • Best Budget Option: Broom finish with integral color. Affordable but still attractive and durable.
  • Best for Existing Patios: Staining combined with a light texture refresh. Updates appearance without full replacement cost.
