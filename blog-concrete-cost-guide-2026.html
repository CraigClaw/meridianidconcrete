2026 Concrete Pricing Overview for Idaho

Concrete prices in the Treasure Valley have remained relatively stable heading into 2026, though material costs continue to fluctuate based on fuel prices, demand, and seasonal factors. Below are current per-square-foot ranges for common residential concrete projects:

Standard Concrete Services (Per Square Foot):

  • Concrete Driveway (broom finish): $6–$10
  • Concrete Patio (broom finish): $6–$9
  • Sidewalks & Walkways: $8–$12
  • Stamped Concrete: $10–$18
  • Exposed Aggregate: $12–$18
  • Concrete Slab/Foundation: $5–$8
  • Concrete Repair (patching): $150–$500 per repair
  • Concrete Removal & Disposal: $2–$4 per sq ft

Note: These are typical ranges for the Meridian/Boise area. Your actual quote may vary based on project specifics, site conditions, and current material costs.

Concrete Driveway Costs in 2026

The driveway is the most common concrete project for Idaho homeowners. Here's what to expect:

Two-Car Driveway (approx. 600 sq ft):

  • Standard broom finish: $3,600–$6,000
  • Colored concrete: $4,800–$7,200
  • Stamped concrete: $6,000–$10,800
  • Exposed aggregate: $7,200–$10,800

Three-Car Driveway (approx. 900 sq ft):

  • Standard broom finish: $5,400–$9,000
  • Colored concrete: $7,200–$10,800
  • Stamped concrete: $9,000–$16,200
  • Exposed aggregate: $10,800–$16,200

Additional Driveway Cost Factors:

  • Thickness: Standard residential driveways are 4 inches thick. If you plan to park RVs, boats, or heavy trucks, 5–6 inch thickness adds $1–$2 per sq ft.
  • Reinforcement: Wire mesh (included in most quotes) or rebar (adds $0.50–$1 per sq ft) for extra strength.
  • Slope/Grade: Steep driveways require additional forming and finishing labor.
  • Apron installation: The transition from street to driveway may require city permits and additional costs.
  • Drainage: Installing drains or French drains adds $500–$2,000 depending on complexity.

What Influences Your Concrete Project Cost?

Several factors determine your final quote. Understanding these helps you plan and budget accurately:

1. Project Size: Larger projects typically cost less per square foot due to economies of scale. A 1,000 sq ft driveway costs less per sq ft than a 100 sq ft walkway.

2. Site Preparation: Does your site need demolition, grading, or extensive excavation? Removing old concrete adds $2–$4 per sq ft. Poor soil conditions may require additional base material ($50–$100 per truckload).

3. Accessibility: Can concrete trucks access your site directly? If not, we may need pumps or wheelbarrow placement, adding $300–$800 to the project.

4. Finish Selection: Basic broom finish is most affordable. Decorative options like stamping, staining, or exposed aggregate add $4–$10 per sq ft but significantly enhance appearance.

5. Reinforcement: Standard wire mesh is typically included. Fiber reinforcement adds $0.30–$0.50 per sq ft. Rebar grids add $0.50–$1 per sq ft.

6. Seasonal Timing: Spring and summer are peak seasons. Scheduling in fall or early spring may offer better availability, though concrete pouring is limited when temperatures drop below freezing.

7. Current Material Costs: Concrete prices fluctuate with fuel costs, cement availability, and regional demand. Our quotes are valid for 30 days to protect against price changes.

Financing Your Concrete Project

Quality concrete work is an investment in your property's value and functionality. Many homeowners choose to finance larger projects:

  • Home equity loans/lines: Often offer the lowest interest rates
  • Contractor financing: Some contractors partner with lenders for convenient payment plans
  • Personal loans: Quick approval, no collateral required
  • Credit cards: Practical for smaller projects; consider cards with 0% introductory APR

We recommend getting multiple quotes and comparing financing options before committing. Remember: the lowest price isn't always the best value if it means sacrificing quality or warranty coverage.

How to Get an Accurate Quote

Phone or email estimates can give rough ballpark figures, but accurate quotes require an on-site evaluation. Here's what to expect:

1. Site Visit: A contractor visits your property to assess measurements, slope, soil conditions, access, and existing surfaces.

2. Scope Discussion: You'll review finish options, thickness requirements, reinforcement needs, and any special features.

3. Written Estimate: Reputable contractors provide detailed written quotes breaking down materials, labor, timeline, and payment terms.

4. Timeline: Most quotes are provided within 24–48 hours of the site visit. Complex projects may take longer.

Maximizing Your Concrete Investment

Concrete is a long-term investment — properly installed concrete lasts 25–40 years or more. Here's how to ensure you get maximum value:

  • Choose quality over price: The cheapest quote often cuts corners on base preparation, reinforcement, or finishing.
  • Verify credentials: Ensure your contractor is licensed, insured, and has local references.
  • Ask about warranties: Reputable contractors stand behind their work with warranties on materials and workmanship.
  • Plan for the long term: Slightly upgrading finish or thickness now prevents costly repairs or replacement later.
  • Consider property value: Quality concrete work typically returns 70–80% of cost in added home value at resale.
